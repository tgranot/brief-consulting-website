About

For about ten years, I read the world for a living. I started in intelligence at the Prime Minister's Office in Tel Aviv, then moved into market research and competitive intelligence at Intel, Wix, Meta, and Melio. My job was to make sense of messy, fast-moving information and explain it clearly to people who needed to make decisions.

Along the way I learned things no curriculum teaches: how to find the signal in noise, how to ask a question that actually gets somewhere, how to take something genuinely complex and make it land in a room full of busy executives. I got good at that.

I chose teaching because the classroom is where those skills matter most. I don't just teach English — I bring a decade of real-world thinking into the room. I want my students to read carefully, argue well, stay curious, and see further than they thought they could.
